# Introduction
- Introduce DoHabit, its purpose and target audience.
- Identify direct and indirect stakeholders.
- Explain how each stakeholder is affected by the application's features.
- Support the discussion with evidence.

DoHabit is a habit tracking tool designed to help users work on long-term consistency with daily goals. It allows users to create new habits, select how many times per day they aim to complete said habits, and track, day-by-day, their progress. Each habit is accompanied by a calendar, on which successful days are highlighted, and a streak number shows how many successful days they have had in a row. The visualisation of their successes let the user see short and long-term progress, keeping them engaged, and the game-like goal of maintaining streaks is constructed to help motivate users to stay consistent.

# Stakeholder Analysis
Direct stakeholders:
- users (people who want to build habits)
- developer

Indirect stakeholders:
- friends and family
- employers

## Overview of Stakeholders
DoHabit's direct stakeholders include its users and its devloper. People indirectly affected by the software include users' friends, families and employers who may be impacted by successfully-built postitive habits, particularly those related to self-improvement or work.

## Users
The people most affected by the software are the people using it every day to build habits. For those committed to using the app, its features, if affective, are likely to have a consistent impact on their daily lives. The whole premise of the app is to instill behaviour changes in its users (with the intention of inducing long-term changes in their attitudes towards the pursuit of personal goals).
User behaviours are affected primarilly through the use of streaks and reward pop-ups, both of which are ethically-debateable methods of maintaining consistent user engagement; for some, they may be positive tools for working towards long-term goals whereas for others they may become points of obsession, leading to a mentally unhealthy relationship with the app.

## Developer
The other person directly impacted by the software is its developer. While the software has not been built for profit, they have a responsibility to ensure the app does not utilise exploitative user engagement methods and that user data remains private, protected and secure.

## Users' Friends and Family
Any significant behaviour changes affectively instilled in users will inevitably have some impact on their close friends and family. Those who know them well and see them regularly will likely notice the effects of the app.
This could be a good or bad thing; on one hand, a user may have used DoHabit to successfully develop certain habits in an effort towards self-improvement, such as making sure they compliment someone each day, resulting in a positive impact on those close to them, or even those more distant. On the other hand, a user may, at first, find the app to be useful but then develop an obsession with maintaining their habit streaks, perhaps drawing them away from loved ones in relentless pursuit of a higher number, with the loss of a streak potentially having a noticeably negative and sudden impact on their mental health, and by extension their attitude towards the people around them.
